## T5-A — is the "owning bullet" objectively and uniquely identifiable?

First-hand re-derivation from the frozen texts alone:

- The new rule's SUBJECT is the retry wrapper: "A retry wrapper must cap
  total elapsed time, not only attempt count … Declare both the attempt
  cap and the wall-clock cap at the call site." Both declared quantities
  are properties of the retry LOOP, and the sentence's own contrast ("not
  only attempt count") extends exactly what the "Retries are bounded and
  jittered" bullet already owns ("declares its maximum attempt count at
  the call site").
- The competing "Timeouts are explicit" bullet scopes per-CALL properties
  ("the call site states its per-attempt timeout"). A wall-clock cap over
  the whole loop is not a per-attempt property; the rule itself cites the
  per-attempt timeout only as the thing that is NOT sufficient.
- Conclusion: under a full reading of both bullets, ownership IS
  analyzable and lands on "Retries are bounded and jittered" — the frozen
  rubric's designation is reasoned, not arbitrary. "Timeouts are explicit"
  is a surface-plausible competitor (the rule's vocabulary — elapsed time,
  timeout, wall-clock — overlaps that bullet's lexical field) but loses on
  scope analysis. The fixture's cross-domain vocabulary makes the test
  hard; it does not make it unfair, and rules that straddle lexical
  domains are exactly the real-world case the clause exists for.
- The doctrine additionally provides its own ambiguity outlet: "Genuinely
  unclear whether it is this bullet's topic or a neighbor's → flag for the
  §6 reviewer rather than defaulting to standalone." None of the six runs
  used it — every run placed confidently (ruled: folded into the
  non-owning bullet 3/3; bare: standalone 2/3, non-owning fold 1/3). The
  observed failure is confident mis-ownership, not experienced ambiguity.

Answer: ownership is analyzable — under the frozen rubric and the scope
analysis above (not claimed as intrinsically objective) — and Retries
wins; this is
therefore NOT primarily a fixture/rubric-ambiguity case, and the doctrine's
ownership structure is not shown defective.

## T5-B — at which layer did the clause fail?

Mechanism classification against the five candidates:

1. Doctrine ownership structure wrong — NO (T5-A: ownership analyzable;
   the rubric's owner designation withstands re-derivation).
2. Structure reasonable but "the host bullet that owns it" lacks
   disambiguation guidance for cross-domain rules — **LIVE.** The clause
   says "read the target section's bullets in full" and offers the
   flag-if-unclear outlet, but gives no positive tie-breaker (e.g.
   ownership follows the rule's constrained SUBJECT, not shared
   vocabulary). Whether adding such guidance would change behavior is
   exactly what current evidence cannot show.
3. Fixture artificially manufactures competing ownership — NO as stated:
   the competing pull is real-world-shaped and the test fair (T5-A);
   "artificial" overstates it. The fixture's difficulty is however part of
   why the failure concentrated here — recorded as context, not as the
   primary mechanism.
4. Executor-specific semantic association (wall-clock → timeout) — **LIVE
   and the most economical single explanation** of the observed 6/6
   directional pull (every placement gravitated to the Timeouts
   neighborhood, bare arms included), but unproven as the SOLE mechanism:
   a single tier, a single fixture, and six same-direction runs cannot
   separate a tier-bound lexical habit from a guidance gap the clause
   could close.
5. Insufficient evidence to distinguish — YES, precisely between (2) and
   (4), and that distinction is decision-relevant: (4) alone ⇒ no doctrine
   work (tier-bound limitation, recorded); (2) ⇒ a candidate doctrine
   ENHANCEMENT (a subject-over-vocabulary tie-breaker) worth an
   owner-gated look. Hence the primary classification.

## What a future probe would need to distinguish (recommendation only — not designed, not executed here)

Decision-relevant uncertainty: does an explicit ownership tie-breaker in
the clause change placement behavior, and does the mis-ownership persist
across tiers/fixtures? A discriminating design would vary (a) clause
wording with/without a subject-over-vocabulary tie-breaker, and/or (b)
executor tier, and/or (c) additional cross-domain fixtures with
pre-registered unique owners — under the pack's standard preregistration
discipline. Whether to open such a campaign is entirely a future owner
decision.
